What four agent builders and two competitors tell us — and the customer questions we cannot answer yet

Three weeks of hands-on product research and desk work on the AI-for-accounting landscape. The useful finding is not a feature gap. It is that these products disagree about what the user is even creating, and that the two competitors closest to Xero are making bets nobody has validated with customers.

Section 00How to read this

Every claim below carries a status and an evidence ID. That is the whole method: raw evidence is immutable and additive, interpretations are living and get overwritten. The status tells you how much weight a line can bear.

Verified Observed firsthand — hands-on, screenshots, product UI.
Docs First-party docs or help centre. Specific enough to act on, not observed.
Claimed Vendor marketing or an exec quote. May be roadmap. Do not build on it.
Unseen Not looked at yet. Stated rather than inferred.
Disputed Sources conflict, or a vendor claim is contradicted by observation.

The vocabulary is enforced, not aspirational. validate fails the corpus if an evidence file is edited after the fact, if a competitor has evidence but no scorecard, if a scorecard does not cite every piece of its own evidence, or if a status outside that list appears. Adding evidence mechanically forces the scorecard to move.

The honest caveat, up front

Confidence across the whole corpus is low to medium. Three products were used hands-on for one pass each. Two competitors are desk research only — no trial, no hands-on. HubSpot was skipped for lack of access, and Karbon's AI Agents product has never been seen by anyone on this side.

Most importantly: there is no customer evidence in the corpus at all. Everything here is about what vendors built and said. Section 03 is about closing that gap, and it is the part I most want a decision on.

Section 01Agent builders: the split is delegation, not features

Notion, Viktor, and Zapier all sell configured automation rather than open-ended autonomy. What separates them is how much of the execution plan the user writes versus hands to the model — and that choice cascades into setup, governance, and who can safely operate the thing.

The axis that actually matters

← More explicit workflow control More delegated agent autonomy →
Workflow Zapier

Many workflows, no agent object. The user owns an explicit graph — steps, inputs, outputs, test, publish — and AI lives inside a step.

Agent Notion

Many named agents, one per job or vertical. Instructions, context, tools, triggers, permissions — but no step graph. A real middle, not a compromise.

Task Viktor

One employee, many tasks. The employee is singular and pre-existing, so the object you create is a task assigned to it. The plan is neither visible nor editable.

The comparison only holds if it is object-to-object. Viktor markets an "AI employee," but that is the persona, not the thing you make — and the count is the discriminator. Notion gives you many agents; Viktor gives you one employee and many tasks; Zapier gives you no agent at all.

That also explains the Viktor result better than "the portal was buggy": there was no task editor because the task was never materialised outside Slack. Hands-on, Zapier published Slack Feedback → AI Triage Analysis → Email. Notion published a Customer Feedback Triage Agent. Viktor took a brief and produced nothing inspectable. ev-agent-zapier-001, ev-agent-notion-003, ev-agent-viktor-003

Why the object choice is the decision, not a UI preference

Permissions, audit, and ownership all attach to whatever the user creates. So "one employee, many tasks" and "many scoped agents" have different governance ceilings before a single feature is built — you cannot scope access per job if there is only one worker to scope. A ledger workflow like invoice exception handling is probably unsafe to leave as "the agent works out the steps," which argues against the Viktor end for anything touching money. Choosing this early is free; choosing it late means rebuilding the governance model. ins-005, hyp-005

1. AI has two jobs here, and they are different products

AI builds the workflow, and AI runs inside the workflow. Every vendor ships both and markets them as one thing.

At build time, Zapier's Copilot interrogates you with clarifying questions and then constructs the Zap; Notion generates an agent from a description that you then edit. At runtime, AI by Zapier is a step in the published graph, and the Notion agent executes against its instructions. You can see the seam where build-time AI is incomplete: Copilot could say what instructions belonged in a step but sometimes needed you to copy and paste them yourself. ev-agent-zapier-001, ev-agent-notion-001

The trust profiles are not comparable. A wrong build-time suggestion is caught by a human before publish. A wrong runtime action posts to the ledger. So build time is the cheaper place to be aggressive — it can be confidently wrong at low cost — provided it produces a reviewable artifact.

This is the real Viktor lesson

Viktor collapsed build time and runtime into one chat. The spec was never materialised as an artifact, so there was nothing to edit, test, or audit — inspectability, testing, and audit were all lost in a single design move, not three. That is the failure mode to avoid, and it is a stronger reason to keep a materialised object than any preference about canvases. ins-012

2. Safety is three separate gaps — and gating is the one nobody made intelligent

These get bundled as "trust and safety." They are three roadmap items with different owners.

GapWhere the three products landedStatus
Auditability
what happened		 Zapier is ahead — Zap history is a first-class page. Notion advertises activity logs that were not on this workspace. Viktor showed 0 tasks while insisting a job was live in the backend. Disputed
Reversibility
undo it		 Absent from all three. No unsend in Notion, no rollback observed anywhere in the category. Verified
Gating
when to ask a human		 All three treat this as static configuration — a "require confirmation before email" toggle, an optional Human-in-the-Loop step, a Slack Approve / Always-approve button. None decides from confidence, risk, or amount. Verified

Statically-configured gating cannot work for ledger writes. A $40 coding and a $120k journal entry cannot share one toggle. Risk-triggered gating is the thing to build — and it is the specific place where the accounting competitors are ahead rather than merely different: Digits routes low-confidence items to an exception queue, Karbon's checks flag and humans resolve. Both Claimed, neither verified. ins-013, ev-comp-digits-001, ev-comp-aider-003

Reversibility is the second-order requirement and the whole category is missing it. For financial writes it is arguably worth more than audit: audit tells an operator what went wrong, rollback lets them fix it.

The related asymmetry

Dangerous actions get real machinery — OAuth scopes, dropdowns, confirmation-before-send. Whether the agent reasoned correctly gets a free-text box with a "Guardrails" heading. For accounting that is backwards: posting to the wrong account is a judgment error, and no approval toggle catches it. Digits' answer is a verification layer that grades each categorisation against firm history before posting — see section 02. ins-003, hyp-003

3. Integration runs both directions, and we tend to plan for one

Every product integrates other tools inbound. Every product can also be integrated outbound, as a capability inside someone else's workflow builder or agent. Those are different strategies that get collapsed into a single ecosystem bullet.

So there are two questions for Xero, and the second is usually asked first. Does Xero host — does the work happen on our surface, competing with Zapier and Karbon for that position? And is Xero callable — can an agent elsewhere use Xero as a capability?

Being callable is not free. The reasoning, the interface, and the customer relationship all move to the caller, and Xero becomes a commodity capability. But refusing means firms route Xero data through competitors that allow it, and Digits' MCP server is already live for Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor while Karbon announced one in June. Zapier is hedging deliberately — a host with a canvas and a positioned tool layer for external agents. MCP is the mechanism once surface-versus-capability is settled, not a substitute for settling it. ins-008, ev-comp-digits-001, ev-agent-zapier-001

Still open on agent builders
  • Create a Zapier Agent, not a Zap — is it still a graph underneath, and does it produce a build-time artifact? hyp-006, ins-012
  • Any product where the gate is confidence- or amount-triggered rather than configured. We have not found one.
  • Does any product offer undo on a completed write?
  • Notion version history — also missing, or just elsewhere?
  • HubSpot: skipped, no access.

Section 02Competitors: two different fights

Digits and Karbon/Aider are both "AI for accounting" and are not competing with each other. They sit at different layers of the stack, which means they threaten different things — and Digits lists Karbon as a partner, so they can stack.

DigitsDocs

Replaces the general ledger. Explicitly not a plugin on QuickBooks and not a close accelerator sitting beside a ledger. The ledger itself is agentic — there is no agent to name, configure, or share. You connect banks and work exceptions.

Off the define-versus-delegate axis entirely. Closer to "the AI is the product" than anything in Section 01.

Threat

Existential, not adjacent. If a firm migrates, Xero loses the system of record and XeroForce has nothing underneath it. ev-comp-digits-001, ev-comp-digits-004

Karbon / AiderVerified

Practice management on top of someone else's GL. What we have actually seen is an opinionated period-close rules engine across a client portfolio: a dashboard of client × period, exception counts per check category, and line-item resolution.

What they market is a set of role-based AI teammates that we have not observed at all. Those are two different things.

Threat

A lifecycle land-grab. If XeroForce stays inside the ledger, Karbon's claimed scope covers reports, onboarding, tax ops and billing by default. ev-comp-aider-002, ev-comp-aider-003

Digits is making two bets. Only one is about architecture.

These get collapsed constantly, including in their own marketing, and they have very different implications for us.

BetClaimStatusEvidence
Architecture Specialized custom-trained ML — explicitly not general-purpose LLMs — beats an LLM on a legacy ledger for categorization accuracy, latency and cost. Tiered client → firm → global models, trained on $875B of real SMB transactions. Docs digits-002
Cadence Categorize, reconcile, verify and post as transactions arrive, so month-end becomes review rather than a cleanup sprint. They distinguish this from "fast month-end close," which they call a shrunken sprint. Claimed digits-003, -004
Autonomy 95%+ of transactions post without human intervention; low-confidence items route to an exception queue; depreciation and amortization entries are drafted subject to accountant approval. Claimed digits-001, -004
Benchmarks The whitepaper on file tests 2,000 transactions from four Digits businesses; their blog cites 93.5% on 17,792 transactions with all frontier LLMs below 73%. Same paper title, different datasets and results. Production claim is 97.8%. Disputed digits-002, -004

The architecture bet does not prove the cadence bet. A better categorizer could perfectly well run as a month-end job. XeroForce could take the first without the second — ship a much faster close on Xero and leave the cadence alone. Digits' counter is that bolt-on AI suffers "truth drift," because execution and verification live in different systems of record. That is their architecture argument, and it is a real one.

The question I cannot answer, and it is the important one

Does anyone actually want continuous close? Digits' own "stay on month-end if" list concedes a lot: fewer than five clients, clients not asking for current reporting, monthly cadence still fits. ev-comp-digits-004

The parallel I keep coming back to is MSP security tooling, where more frequent scanning is not straightforwardly better — for a thinly-staffed org it is often just more work. A mid-month exception drip may cost more than one contained close if nobody reads Tuesday-afternoon numbers. Digits' story is a client seeing treated numbers the day a Stripe payout lands. That is a good story. It is not evidence.

And "do they want it" is the weaker form of the question. Continuous close is a behaviour migration, not a feature. The firm has to move from batch review to continuous exception handling, which changes staffing, scheduling, and what clients expect. That is why Digits had to rebuild the ledger — and also why a firm might decline even if the technology works perfectly. So: are firms willing to change behaviour, and does adoption need an explicit incentive to happen at all? hyp-011

Why automation goes this far in accounting: verifiability, not an absence of judgment

The tempting read of a 95% auto-post rate is that accounting is rules-based and needs little judgment — which would imply adoption should outrun engineering easily. Digits' own whitepaper contradicts that read. It reports 10.4% human-versus-human disagreement on categorisation and a 79.1% human baseline. Two accountants shown the same transaction disagree roughly one time in ten. A rulebook would put humans near 100% and in agreement with each other. ev-comp-digits-002

The distinction that survives the evidence is verifiability. A categorisation can be graded — against firm history, prior decisions, and a ledger that must balance. That is exactly what lets Digits run a second AI layer checking the first before posting. Engineering has no equivalent grader: tests catch some errors and no design errors.

What this changes about how we think about adoption

Trust is the outcome; demonstrable verification is the mechanism that produces it. So the thing to build is the checking layer, not more model quality — and that is a concrete, fundable answer to "how do we earn permission to act."

But the adoption equation has two terms pulling opposite ways. Verifiability raises the ceiling; consequence severity raises the confidence threshold required before anyone lets an agent act. A bad commit is reverted in review. A bad posting has tax and audit exposure and may need a restatement. So the likely shape is: fast adoption where errors are cheap to correct — coding, categorisation, reconciliation — and slow adoption for anything filed, remitted, or reported externally. That is a customer question, not a desk-research one. ins-014, hyp-012

Karbon: keep "seen" and "marketed" strictly apart

This is where I got it wrong once already, and the correction is instructive. I initially placed Karbon near Zapier on the delegation axis by inferring from the host product. Then their Agents article turned out to sell Viktor-like firm roles — Bookkeeper, Tax Admin, Fractional CFO, Client Onboarding Specialist Claimed. So I moved them.

That was also wrong. The tutorial we have is period close: a portfolio dashboard, one Monthly Close — Accrual firm template applied to 82 clients, checks labelled "Built-in" or "Automatic — N conditions," an exception inbox, and an Ask Client panel bound to a specific transaction that batches questions to a 5pm send Verified. ev-comp-aider-003

Those are rules, and rules are not agents. That tutorial is not evidence of how Karbon AI Agents work. The Agents product, Kai, and the Agent Management System audit trail have never been seen Unseen. So Karbon does not go on the delegation axis at all yet. ins-009

The generalizable signal

The more specific a claim, the more likely it is real. Vague role metaphors — employee, coworker, teammate — are easy to over-read. Notion markets activity logs that are missing; Viktor markets a live task with an empty portal; Karbon markets four roles nobody has seen. Digits' claims are quantified, which at least makes them checkable. Weight shipped, observable behaviour over announced roadmap, and never let competitor marketing language become a product requirement. ins-007

Their tutorials teach workflow and tool together — which cuts both ways

Aider's tutorial names the manager's problem first (no single view of client close; "status lives in weekly meetings and memory"; the same problem at 15 or 15,000 clients), then shows the tool. One Monthly Close — Accrual template sits on 82 clients. Teaching workflow-plus-tool only works if the close is standardised enough to template — the contrast with MSP tooling, where vendors sell tools and workflows vary so widely that process content does not generalise. ev-comp-aider-003, ins-010

Low process variance is good for shipping and bad for defensibility, and only the first half usually gets noticed. Templates work and onboarding is fast — but if one accrual template covers 82 clients, that is as easy for Karbon to copy from us as for us to copy from them. There is no moat in a standard process.

Two cautions on the inference. A template on 82 clients shows one firm standardised, not an industry. And a vendor teaching the close as standard has a commercial interest in it being standard. The stronger argument is from the content market rather than from Karbon: tutorials that teach process would not find an audience if practitioners did not recognise the process.

Both competitors are further along than the agent builders on trust and reach

Their trust models are ahead of the agent builders'

Digits claims a separate AI verification layer that checks each categorization against firm history and standards before posting, with named exception flags — missing, date mismatch, duplicate, overmatched. Karbon markets approve/edit/reject with a complete audit trail of every agent decision. Both are more concrete than Notion's missing activity logs, Viktor's empty portal, or Zapier's optional HITL step — though both are Claimed, not verified.

If XeroForce copies agent-builder trust patterns rather than competitor trust patterns, we will underdeliver on accountability for financial writes. hyp-008

Both ship or announce MCP servers

Digits' is live for Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor; Karbon announced one in June. The value is shifting from "our AI is best" to "our data is reachable" — which is the outbound half of the integration question in section 01, and it is being answered in the market whether or not we answer it deliberately. ins-008

The strategic question this section raises

Is XeroForce defending the ledger against Digits, or covering the client lifecycle Karbon is claiming — reports, onboarding, tax, advisory, billing? Staying on "agentic workflows inside the ledger" answers the first and concedes the second by default. ins-006, hyp-009

Section 03What I want the engine to do next: customer validation

Sections 01 and 02 are vendor-facing. The corpus has 16 evidence files and zero customer evidence, which means every strategic question above currently terminates in "ask a customer." The harness was built for this; it has not been pointed at it yet.

Where the research stands

DomainStateGap
Agent builders 3 products, one hands-on pass each. 9 evidence files. Zapier Agents unseen. HubSpot skipped. Second passes not done.
Competitors 2 competitors, desk research. 7 evidence files, 2 live scorecards. No hands-on trial for either. Karbon Agents never seen.
Customers Empty. Everything. This is the blocker.
Product / accounting context Directories exist, unfilled. No Xero-internal grounding yet.

The useful property we already have

Every hypothesis in the corpus carries an explicit what_would_strengthen and what_would_falsify. That is not documentation — it is an interview guide that already exists. Twelve hypotheses currently generate roughly seventy specific things that would move a belief. The engine's job is to turn those into questions, absorb the answers, and show which beliefs moved.

The cheapest first input is not new interviews

Existing customer call recordings. That evidence already exists, needs no recruitment, and can be mined against the falsifiers above before a single new conversation is scheduled. It reorders this whole section: ingest and code what we already have, find out which beliefs it already moves, and only then design interviews for the gaps it cannot close. The main ask becomes access, not calendar time.

What I want the engine to do

  1. Generate the interview guide from the beliefs, not by hand. Falsifiers become questions. If a hypothesis cannot produce a question a customer could answer, it is not really a hypothesis.
  2. Ingest customer evidence under the same immutability rule. Interview notes and quotes become evidence files that are never rewritten, so a later reinterpretation cannot quietly launder itself into the record.
  3. Make falsification a first-class output. I want a standing list of beliefs that customer evidence killed. Right now the outputs only accumulate. A research system that never reports a dead hypothesis is telling you something about itself.
  4. Track belief movement over time. Which insight changed confidence, on which date, because of which evidence ID. The competitor scorecards do this with changelogs; beliefs should too.
  5. Extend the status discipline to every domain. Competitors have enforced scorecards. Agent builders and customers do not — see the backlog note. Customer evidence especially needs the "one firm said this" versus "we heard this eight times" distinction to be structural rather than remembered.
Success criterion

After a round of customer conversations, I should be able to answer "what changed?" from generated output rather than from memory — and at least one belief in this deck should be dead. If nothing dies, the questions were not sharp enough.

The five questions I would take to customers first

Each maps to a live hypothesis, and each has a real chance of killing something in this deck.

Ordered by how much a clear answer would change what XeroForce builds.
QuestionWhat we would learnBelief
Do you want current numbers, or a clean month-end? Tests Digits' cadence bet directly, using Digits' own cut. Do firms ignore intra-month flags? Do clients ever ask for mid-period treated books, or is that a SaaS story? If a faster month-end is enough, we can ignore continuous close and cover most of the market Digits concedes. hyp-011
Does your close look like other firms' closes? Aider pushes one accrual template onto 82 clients, so the close must be standardized enough to template. If that holds we can be genuinely opinionated about close. If firms have heavily custom processes, they will reject templates the way MSPs reject one-size playbooks. hyp-010
What would you let run without looking at it? Locates the real appetite on the delegation axis for financial writes, rather than us guessing a point on it. Likely varies sharply by action type — which is itself the answer. hyp-005
After an agent acts, what do you need to see — and be able to undo? Whether a run log is table stakes or a nice-to-have, and whether rollback is expected. Competitors market embedded verification and audit trails; agent builders do not ship them; we do not know which customers actually pay for. hyp-003, hyp-008, ins-013
Which parts of the close would you let run if a check verified them first? Tests the verifiability thesis and separates it from blanket distrust. The prediction is that reversible steps (coding, reconciliation) get automated readily and anything filed or remitted does not. What I need to get any of this

Access to customers or to existing Xero customer research, and a view of what XeroForce's own brief already covers. That last one matters: whether the brief includes client reports and lifecycle operations, or stops at ledger workflows, decides whether hyp-009 is a real risk or already handled.

Section 04Asks and next steps

Decisions I would like input on

  1. Which fight are we in? Defending the ledger against Digits, or covering the client lifecycle against Karbon, or both. This shapes everything downstream, and it is the one question here we can answer internally without customer research — which makes it both the cheapest to resolve and the most blocking. ins-006, hyp-009
  2. What object does XeroForce give the user — workflow, agent, or task? Not a UI question. Permissions, audit, and ownership attach to whatever they create, so this sets the governance ceiling before any feature exists. ins-005, hyp-005
  3. Is Xero a surface or a capability? Do we want work to happen on our platform, be callable from someone else's agent, or both like Zapier? MCP investment follows this answer rather than substituting for it. ins-008
  4. Can I get access to existing customer call recordings? This is the unblocking ask. It is cheaper than running interviews, the evidence already exists, and without it section 03 stays theoretical.

What I will do regardless

  • Mine existing customer calls against the falsifiers already in the corpus, as soon as I have access.
  • Create a Zapier Agent rather than a Zap — the cleanest single test of whether the workflow graph survives, and of whether it produces a build-time artifact. hyp-006, ins-012
  • Hands-on trials of Digits and Aider period close, to convert Claimed rows into Verified or Disputed ones — starting with what actually routes an item into Digits' exception queue, since risk-triggered gating is the capability we most need to understand.
  • Reconcile Digits' conflicting benchmark figures.
  • Extend the enforced scorecard pattern to agent builders and to the customer domain, so the next corpus starts with the discipline rather than acquiring it later.
One thing to take away

The competitors are not ahead of us on AI capability. They are ahead on having picked a position — Digits on architecture and cadence, Karbon on scope. The risk for XeroForce is not shipping a worse agent. It is shipping an unpositioned one, and I do not think we can pick the position honestly until we have talked to customers about cadence and autonomy.
