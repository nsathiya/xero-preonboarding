# Digits vs Xero page: continuous close as the differentiator

## Raw evidence

Page: [Digits vs. Xero](https://digits.com/compare/xero/)
(captured 2026-09-03). Competitor comparison marketing.

Banner: "Connect your firm stack to Digits—New partnerships
with: Ignition, **Karbon**, Reach Reporting."

Price frame: **Digits Core $100/mo** vs **Xero Established
$90/mo**. "Features change—verify on vendor pages."

### How Digits describes itself vs Xero

Digits:

> AI-native accounting platform that reconciles to bank
> statements automatically, turns financials into live
> dimensional dashboards, and unlocks unlimited
> collaboration—no rules, no scripts, no seat limits.

> AI isn’t a feature; it’s the foundation. Digits is
> AI-native from day one, powered by the Agentic General
> Ledger™. Built for machine learning and trained on 170M+
> real transactions, Digits auto-books, reconciles, and
> reviews continuously, then surfaces only the exceptions
> that need a human.

Xero (Digits' characterization):

> AI assistance added on top of traditional workflows. Xero
> relies on bank rules plus AI-powered suggestions, but
> users still review and confirm actions to keep books
> accurate.

### Continuous vs queue (Digits' contrast)

| Topic | Digits says about itself | Digits says about Xero |
|---|---|---|
| Bank rec | Continuous AI rec; flags only what needs review | A reconcile **queue**, not exception flags; bank rules + AI suggestions still need review |
| Exceptions | Clear flags (Missing, Date mismatch, Duplicate, Overmatched) + audit trail | Same "queue, not flags" line |
| Close cadence | As soon as statements arrive, rec draft is generated; finalize in a few clicks | Transactions imported each business day; get close-ready by working the queue, then running close reports |
| Controls | AI does routine work; you supervise exceptions; most activity auto-booked | Human-driven accuracy; users still review/confirm and maintain rules |
| Effort | Minimal ongoing; 24/7 AI bookkeeping; drop in a statement | Automation reduces clicks but you still do the work; regular review/coding, rule upkeep, ongoing rec |

FAQ — "How is Digits different from Xero?":

> Xero is a traditional accounting system with workflows
> driven by human input. Digits is an AI-native, agentic
> platform that continuously handles the work of
> bookkeeping — so accountants can focus on judgment,
> review, and advisory work.

FAQ — firms: Xero "was built around workflows that depend on
people to move work forward." Digits "continuously runs the
routine accounting work." "In a traditional system, like
Xero, each new client adds more manual work. In Digits, each
correction helps improve performance across the platform."

FAQ — migration: some Xero plans have more limited access to
underlying data; migration may be more hands-on.

"Which platform is right for you?" — Digits for teams that
want to "stop babysitting automation—no rule building, no
rule tuning, just exceptions when needed." Xero "may be
better for" teams that prefer hands-on bookkeeping, are
comfortable with rules-based automation, and will stitch
marketplace add-ons.

Other Digits claims on the page: unlimited users on a
per-business plan; built-in comments / Inbox; departments +
locations included; 12,000+ banks via Plaid; built-in bill
pay and invoicing (vs Hubdoc as a connected product on
Xero). Testimonial: Jackie Anthony, AllCents Consulting —
"Digits saves 6 hours per client."

### Researcher question (not a Digits claim)

What is the benefit of **continuous** close / reconciliation
to the end customer? Digits' answer is time saved and more
automation. But you could automate the same work toward
month-end. Why can't Xero be more automated **at** close
without changing cadence?

MSP parallel: not every org wants higher-frequency security
scans. More frequent scans can mean more work for teams with
fewer people. Continuous exception flags might do the same
if nobody needs intra-period books, or if the flags are
noisy.

This needs customer evidence. Do not treat Digits' cadence
as an observed user want.

## Notes

Digits is not only saying "we categorize better." The
comparison page makes **cadence** the product: continuously,
as activity happens, vs Xero's daily import + reconcile
queue + close reports.

Two separable bets:

1. **Architecture** (`ev-comp-digits-002`): specialized AGL
   vs LLM-on-top-of-a-traditional-GL. About accuracy,
   latency, cost.
2. **Cadence** (this page): work the exceptions all month vs
   batch at month-end. About when humans show up.

(1) does not prove (2). A better categorizer could still run
as a month-end job. Continuous close only pays if someone
uses always-current books, or if spreading exceptions is
actually less work than one pile. Digits asserts both. Not
verified.

Also: Digits lists **Karbon as a partner** on this page.
GL-replacement and practice-layer can sit together. That
softens a pure Digits-vs-Karbon fight (`ins-006`) — a firm
could keep Karbon and move the ledger to Digits.

Follow-ups:

- Ask accountants / SMB operators: do you want exceptions
  during the month, or a heavier but contained close?
- When do they actually look at intra-period P&L / cash?
- Does a noisy continuous inbox feel like the MSP "more
  scans = more tickets" problem?
- Hands-on: how many exception flags per week vs Xero rec
  queue at month-end?
